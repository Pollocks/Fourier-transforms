Unshifted even functions have real, even Fourier transforms. The finite sine wave on [0,L] has a complex Fourier transform because it is not even about x = 0. A shifted Gaussian has a complex transform with phase −kx₀. Negative sinc lobes are shown below the axis in amplitude mode. All transforms are analytic (not numerical FFT approximations). The two horizontal scales remain fixed to make width changes directly visible. Both plots use a fixed zero-line position, with three quarters of the plotting height above zero and one quarter below, even in probability-density mode.

For a quantum wavefunction, the Normalize button sets ∫|f(x)|² dx = ∫|f̃(k)|² dk = 1. The separate delta-function mode instead sets ∫f(x) dx = 1, At zero width, δ(x) and its constant Fourier transform are distributions/generalized functions, not ordinary normalizable wavefunctions.
